\begin{abstract}
An initial C-SQD deployment can operate the communication service and its
canonical ledger while one configured payment-processing arrangement executes annual utility charges,
request-specific charges, refunds, and member payments. This profile follows
a service purchase and a relationship request through to annual member allocation
and automatic lump-sum payment to each member's associated bank account. It defines the
C-SQD financial program, then explains the operational, privacy, and migration
choices needed to deliver it. Company ownership of the pool does not make its
member-restricted portion available for ordinary corporate spending.
\end{abstract}

\section{What This Profile Controls}

The \textit{Protocol Specification} defines permission, request outcomes, and
the distinction between committed obligations and completed external payments.
Section~\ref{sec:program}, the \emph{C-SQD Financial Program}, adds normative
requirements for deployments advertising this profile. In that section,
``must'' and ``must not'' are requirements. The remaining sections are deployment
guidance. Values explicitly marked proposed are planning defaults, not approved
live-payment terms.

The companion \href{DOMAIN_MODEL.md}{C-SQD domain model} records the confirmed
product rules and deferred decisions. Implementation choices do not override
those clarifications. In this profile, a communication
\emph{provider} operates the mail service; a payment \emph{processor} executes
financial operations. The current product assumes one configured
payment-processing arrangement, not routing among multiple processors.

The financial model separates three events: an annual charge purchases service,
a conditional charge supports a relationship request, and the member program
returns eligible pooled forfeitures through annual allocations. None creates a
spendable account balance. The legal characterization and processing arrangement
still require assessment; these labels do not establish a money-transmission
exemption.

\section{A Request from Charge to Outcome}

Alice has no standing permission to write to Bob. She selects one of Bob's
published request classes and reviews its fixed quote,
authorizes $C+S$, and supplies her initial encrypted communication. C-SQD records
one request and asks the external provider to capture that charge under a stable
operation key. Once funding satisfies the published finality policy and content is durable,
submission places the request before Bob and starts its decision clock.

Bob's follow-up setting governs additional messages during that clock. If he
permits them, Alice may add messages under the same request. They cost no more,
do not move the deadline, and do not generate another request prompt. If he does
not permit them, C-SQD refuses the follow-up without selling another charge
against the pending request.

Acceptance records a full refund obligation together with either standing
directed permission or a relationship-level express lane. Rejection
records processing revenue $C$ and pending forfeiture $S$. Silence through expiry
records $C$ as revenue and a refund obligation for $S$. Bob receives no personal
entitlement from rejecting Alice. Each obligation remains visible until the
external execution is reconciled, even if Alice or Bob closes an account.

Funding and submission cannot form one database transaction with a payment
provider. Recovery must therefore complete the story. A cancelled or timed-out
request being prepared is not submitted when delayed funding evidence arrives;
the captured amount is refunded in full. An uncertain capture is queried under
its original key rather than retried as another charge. A failed refund remains
an obligation and enters retry or review.

\section{C-SQD Financial Program}\label{sec:program}

\subsection{The annual utility charge purchases service}

C-SQD must publish an annual utility price and collect the full charge well
before the specified year-long service period begins. An anticipated or finalized
pool allocation must not reduce that charge at collection. The later distribution
is a separate bank payment; it must not shorten or revoke purchased service.

Published service terms must fix the price, settlement unit, collection date,
and year-long service interval. A failed collection and its retry must apply to
that same interval and price. A late payment must not shift or extend the period.
Policy changes apply to future offers, not an existing purchase. Exact calendar
or anniversary anchors and the advance collection lead time remain policy
decisions; this profile does not invent a universal date or offset. Collection
date, service interval, pool year, and distribution date are distinct facts.

New service coverage requires authenticated evidence that funding has met the
provider's finality policy. Pending funding grants no new coverage, and late
funding grants no retroactive coverage. Expiry of existing coverage prevents new
communication through the local service, including accepted and lane messages;
it does not erase relationships, revoke lanes, prevent recipient decisions, or
cancel refunds and member payables. A remote sender's service entitlement belongs
to that sender's provider.

A billing account and a verified distribution member serve different purposes.
An account may purchase service without enrolling in the member program. A member
may retain an annual allocation after choosing not to renew service.

\subsection{Request pricing is fixed at authorization}

The standard request quote combines processing amount $C=\$0.50$ with a
recipient-selected collateral amount $S$ for the chosen request class. The current default is $S=\$5.00$;
the bounded menu is $\$1$, $\$2.50$, $\$5$, $\$10$, and $\$25$. The deployment
versions the processing amount, menu, and default. A recipient's authenticated
preference must select an allowed value for each class. Recipients define the
classes and their descriptions; C-SQD does not prescribe purpose-based prices.
The sender's selected class and financial terms must remain fixed for the request.
A later preference or policy change must not alter that request's signed quote.
Both timely acceptance modes must record the full $C+S$ refund obligation with
the granted permission; subsequent lane expiry or revocation must not reopen it.

\subsection{Ownership and the limits on its use}

C-SQD is the service operator and owner of the company pool in this profile.
It is responsible for request refunds and finalized member payments, while an
external provider executes the underlying transactions. The live policy must
identify the contracting entity, merchant/payment responsibilities, and applicable
processing arrangement before charges begin.

The pool has no participant ownership shares and no earmarked claim linking a
particular rejected request to its recipient. Before annual allocation, a
member has no individually finalized claim to a fraction of its unallocated
funds. At allocation, the ledger records an individual payment obligation for
each qualifying member. This accounting distinction must be reflected in the
published program terms rather than inferred from the word ``pool.''

Eligible forfeitures undergo one disclosed corporate allocation. The remainder
is restricted to member distributions. C-SQD must not spend that remainder on
operations, payout fees, or corporate losses, or assess it again in later
years. Annual utility charges, retained processing revenue, and the corporate
allocation support those company costs. The restriction permits no further
undisclosed deductions from member funds.

\subsection{When a forfeiture becomes eligible}

Rejection is the beginning of the forfeiture process, not permission to distribute
cash. Each pending lot must retain a scoped request cause, original payment
reference, amount, unit, rate/policy version, maturity evidence, and assessment
status. Before eligibility, the policy's capture/settlement, refund, dispute,
and reserve conditions must be satisfied. Refund obligations and unresolved
amounts must remain separately accounted for and unavailable to the pool.

Year end alone does not establish payment finality. A lot that fails maturity
at cutoff stays pending until a later eligible year; it does not receive an
estimated member allocation. A hold or dispute must identify the affected lot
and its review condition. Reserve methodology, release conditions, and later-loss
funding must be approved before live distribution, not invented by a worker.

C-SQD must record later external reversals through authorized compensating
entries and fund the resulting obligations without silently taking restricted
member funds or cancelling finalized payables. A final request outcome remains
part of the journal even when a financial correction is necessary.

\subsection{The corporate allocation happens once}

The current corporate-rate default is $\alpha=0.03$. The adopted rate and rounding
policy must be disclosed through immutable request terms. A future rate change
applies prospectively; the rate at a later maturity date does not replace the
one attached to an existing request.

For allocation year $y$, group newly eligible, previously unassessed lots by settlement
unit and quoted rate version $v$. Let $F_{yv}$ be a group's amount in integer
minor units. The corporate amount and new member contribution are
\[
G_y=\sum_v\left\lfloor\alpha_v F_{yv}\right\rfloor,
\qquad M_y=\sum_v F_{yv}-G_y.
\]
The floor is applied once per rate cohort and unit at the finalized annual
boundary, with any fractional corporate unit left for members. Each contributing
lot must be marked assessed in the same atomic allocation record. Replays cannot
assess it again. Calculations must use checked integer or exact rational
arithmetic, never floating-point currency.

For example, $10{,}000$ units of newly eligible forfeitures at 3\% produce $300$
of corporate revenue and $9{,}700$ for members. This assessment excludes $C$,
refund obligations and unresolved request amounts. Previously assessed member funds and finalized unpaid allocations are
also excluded. The sender is not charged an additional 3\%.

\subsection{Who qualifies for an equal share}

The program benefits qualifying active members generally. It must not weight a
share by requests received, collateral contributed, rejections, messages sent,
or correspondence with a particular sender. One qualifying member receives one
share, irrespective of aliases, mailboxes, or devices.

The distribution identity is distinct from the protocol's private principal and
public communication identities. The program must prevent duplicate enrollment
while keeping identity evidence out of delivery, relationship, and telemetry
records. Eligibility requires program opt-in, the published membership/activity
conditions, and required identity checks. Confirmed abuse or duplicate membership
must be handled under a disclosed review and correction procedure.

The proposed initial eligibility policy is membership for at least 30 days by
cutoff and intentional authenticated use on at least two distinct days of the
year. Reading can count. Background synchronization, automated polling, and
incoming lane traffic alone do not establish activity. Store minimal activity
evidence, not message content or a history of correspondents. The current
C-SQD account model is personal: one identifiable person, one account, potentially
multiple email addresses, with identity tied to a verified bank account.
Organizational account models are outside this product scope. Renewal for the following service
year must not be an eligibility condition. Eligibility is determined from the
completed allocation year, independently of the member's billing account.

\subsection{From the annual cutoff to fixed payables}

Allocations occur once per calendar year. The program must publish its eligibility
policy version and cutoff rule before the period. Year $y$ is the half-open
interval from January 1 of $y$ to January 1 of $y+1$, in UTC; an event at the
ending boundary belongs to the new year. A review interval after cutoff can
resolve already recorded evidence without admitting later activity into the
closed year.

At finalization, fix the qualifying membership snapshot and eligible funding
manifest. Let $K_{y-1}$ be unallocated, previously assessed member carryforward,
and let $N_y$ be the qualifying member count. Together with the newly assessed
member contribution $M_y$, the amount available is $D_y=K_{y-1}+M_y$.
For $N_y>0$,
\[
a_y=\left\lfloor D_y/N_y\right\rfloor,
\qquad K_y=D_y-N_y a_y.
\]
Each member receives a payable of $a_y$ minor units. The remainder $K_y$ remains
restricted carryforward. If $N_y=0$, no individual allocation is created and
$K_y=D_y$. An empty pool creates no payment. Settlement units must be handled
separately; no implicit foreign exchange is permitted.

The snapshot, manifest, assessment marks, payables, remainder, and journal entries
must finalize as one visible outcome. An operator must not silently rerun the
year with a different membership set. Corrections require a separate authorized
record with a stated cause and funding source; they do not overwrite the
finalized allocation.

\subsection{The member's annual distribution date}

Finalization establishes each member's share of the previous calendar year's
company-wide collateral pool. One ordinary annual distribution covers that share.
Published distribution terms set its due date separately from service collection
and coverage. Ordinarily distribution occurs during a service year whose utility
charge was already paid. Renewal for the next year, and a pending or failed
collection for that year, must not delay, reclassify, or cancel the distribution.
This profile does not require payment a fixed number of days after collection.

A due date is an earliest execution date, not a guarantee of processor completion
that day. After finalization and when due, C-SQD automatically forwards the
distribution to the verified bank account associated with the member's account.
No separate per-distribution instructions or independently chosen destination
are required. Closure must preserve a minimal route for outstanding distributions
and refunds. Detailed account management and bank replacement remain deferred.

\subsection{One payment, with rebate and excess reporting}

Let $D_i$ be member $i$'s finalized allocation and $U$ the annual utility charge
(\$120 in the clarified example). The reporting classifications are
\[
R_i=\min(D_i,U),\qquad X_i=\max(0,D_i-U),\qquad R_i+X_i=D_i.
\]
$R_i$ is the utility-fee rebate/refund classification and $X_i$ is the excess
cash classification. They are bookkeeping and reporting distinctions within
\emph{one lump-sum bank payment}. C-SQD must not implement them as an original-charge
refund and a separate payout. Remaining processor-refundable capacity does not
enter the calculation, and future renewal status does not change $U$.

For $U=\$120$, allocations of \$80, \$120, and \$170 produce single payments
of those amounts, with rebate/excess breakdowns of \$80/\$0, \$120/\$0,
and \$120/\$50 respectively. A zero allocation needs no external payment.
Actual relationship-request refunds retain their capture-linked refund meaning.

The distribution has one obligation and one payment lifecycle, including its
sequential attempts and authenticated evidence. An uncertain response must be
reconciled under the original operation identity. A definite failure may permit
a new attempt for the same obligation. A later reversal preserves evidence and
restores the unpaid obligation for reconciliation; it must not silently trigger
an unrelated payment or erase the allocation.

Failed or delayed payments stay assigned to their members, outside the next
year's equal split. Account closure and year rollover do not erase them.
C-SQD must not deduct payout fees from the stated allocation. No member-selected
payout minimum, cross-year aggregation, or payment-threshold rule is adopted here.

Statements show the full annual utility charge, finalized allocation, rebate
and excess classifications, and one distribution completion status. There is
no spendable cs-mail balance, transfer to another member, or automatic netting
against the next service purchase. The distribution leaves funded coverage intact.

\subsection{Published policy and program closure}

The launch policy must publish the adopted corporate rate, eligibility thresholds,
maturity and reserve criteria, service periods and advance collection terms,
distribution terms, and review timelines. It must also define account closure, unclaimed obligations, residual
restricted funds, and orderly program termination before collecting real value.
Stopping the program cannot by itself convert member-restricted funds into
unrestricted corporate revenue or extinguish unpaid obligations.

The program should publish aggregate reconciliation: new eligible forfeitures,
corporate allocation, member contribution, carryforward, qualifying member count,
finalized payables, and payment completion. Individual correspondence or the
amount a particular recipient rejected must not be disclosed through those
reports.

\section{Operating the Service Without a Wallet}

C-SQD can initially run one provider and ledger, but identity, relationship,
ciphertext, accounting, membership verification, and telemetry need distinct
credentials, keys, and access roles. The payment adapter receives scoped payment
references and required amounts. It does not need message content, a recipient's
contact graph, or events for every follow-up message.

The operational ledger should reconcile utility-service receipts, request funding,
unresolved requests, refunds,
pending forfeitures, corporate revenue, restricted funds, and member payables
against external statements. An operator investigates mismatches through narrowly
authorized records and compensating commands, not arbitrary balance edits.
Unknown outcomes stay visible until resolved. A processor outage must not become
a duplicate charge or a claim that a refund has completed.

Funding attempts require C-SQD-controlled capacity shared by the account's request
and utility charges. The current limit bounds simultaneously unresolved attempts,
not lifetime attempts. Failure or reversal restricts the source until authenticated
re-verification; bank replacement policy remains deferred. A queued reservation
does not authorize dispatch: the durable transaction must check current
restrictions before granting a new dispatch. Looking up an uncertain operation under its
original identity is reconciliation; dispatching another pull is a new attempt
and requires available authority and capacity. Users must not bypass restrictions
by creating new requests or changing command identifiers.

Current C-SQD enrollment binds one identifiable person to one account and its
verified bank association, with multiple email aliases permitted. Account actions
require explicit grants checked against active operational keys; communication
permission alone is not financial authority. This does not introduce a separate
user-managed billing credential. Bank, membership, and communication records
retain separate purposes, access, and scoped identifiers. Legacy organizations
may send through express lanes without becoming C-SQD account holders. Future
provider migration must preserve recipient-scoped abuse continuity without
exporting a universal principal identifier.

\section{The Recipient and Sender Experience}

The sender sees the recipient's request classes and their collateral requirements,
then the chosen class, price, and initial communication for one request,
the possible refunds, and the decision window. The interface distinguishes preparation for submission, waiting for the
recipient's decision, and the terminal outcome, with external refund status
shown separately. It does not present available or withdrawable funds. A
request still being prepared offers authenticated cancellation; closing the application
does not imply that the charge was cancelled.

The account view shows the annual service price, coverage interval, next charge
date, and scheduled distribution date. It presents the full charge and later
lump-sum distribution as separate transactions, with the rebate/excess breakdown
as reporting. A member who does not renew can still inspect
and receive a finalized allocation.

The recipient sees the selected class, one grouped request, and choices to accept
with standing permission, accept with an express lane, reject, or block.
The explanation starts with future permission, then the financial consequence.
Rejecting does not earn the recipient that request's collateral. Dismissing the
prompt is not a decision. Existing late standing acceptance does not reopen the
closed request's refund outcome. Bounded-acceptance edge cases remain open
in the domain model.

Follow-up controls belong beside request preferences and are enforced at
admission. Recipients may disable follow-ups or set bounded counts and rates;
changes affect subsequent admissions, not immutable financial terms. Accepted
communication and valid express lanes retain their own authorization paths.

The proposed request policy uses a 30-day decision window, a 30-day cooldown
after expiry, and a 90-day cooldown after rejection. Cooldowns run from the
canonical decision deadline for expiry and the canonical timely rejection/block
event for rejection allocation, subject to any later principal-history bound.
These values remain proposals. Switching classes cannot bypass a pending request
or principal-recipient history. A finalized policy must define recipient-imposed
longer limits, explicit invitations that can shorten ordinary cooldowns, and
behavior after revocation. Blocking always requires an authenticated unblock;
an invitation cannot silently remove it.

\section{SMTP and Express-Lane Migration}

SMTP is a visibly identified compatibility route because its gateway may handle
plaintext. A native message's end-to-end confidentiality must not be claimed for
ordinary gateway-readable mail.

Within the funded service boundary, inbound processing checks blocking, standing
acceptance, and an applicable recipient-granted lane. It then checks whether an authenticated message may join
an identified request awaiting a decision. Only an eligible new request reaches the payment
invitation flow. The web authorization binds the directed parties, request,
selected request class, initial content/declarations, amount, immutable terms, and deadlines. It never
asks the sender to top up a general account.

Subsequent legacy mail must prove its association and satisfy current follow-up
policy; an address or subject-line match alone is insufficient authority. A
failed lane or follow-up cannot silently authorize a new charge. Invitations
need rate limits, anti-enumeration controls, clear expiry, and abuse reporting.
Outbound downgrade must be disclosed before the sender commits to that route.

Express-lane messages carry no request charge or pool contribution. Acceptance
with a lane settles its request; later lane changes do not reopen that outcome.
A lane concerns the relationship and is usually not specific to one conversation.
A separate lane-management grant also resolves a pending request with the full
refund; terminal settlements remain fixed. Domain-authentication evidence
supports the lane's sender scope, not the truth of its declared purpose or a
member's active-use eligibility.

\section{Incentives, Review, and Later Federation}

Equal member distributions remove a direct rejection bounty, but they do not
remove economic incentives. Misclassification faces ordinary rejection, without
a separate penalty or reputation mechanism. C-SQD earns $C$ on unsuccessful requests and a share
of eligible forfeitures. It should publish pricing controls and preserve signed
decision evidence so neither the service nor a recipient can manufacture expiry
by hiding acceptance. Review should address duplicate members, fabricated
activity, abusive request patterns, collusion, and manipulated cutoff evidence.

A single company-owned pool is the initial model. Independent-provider federation
requires a new agreement assigning the merchant, refund debtor, pool owner,
membership authority, duplicate-member controls, clearing, default losses, and
payment responsibility. Inter-provider clearing may net mutual obligations;
it cannot create new ownership rules or turn a rejected sender's collateral into
a direct recipient payout. The centralized draft therefore does not promise that
the former recipient-collateral clearing example transfers unchanged to federation.

\section{A Staged Launch}

Extend the current deterministic model with request classes and acceptance with
a lane, preserving the unresolved interactions listed in the domain model.
Validate durable ordering, double-entry obligations, and recovery through a
simulated external provider. A desktop pilot can use nonredeemable amounts while testing
real encryption, request/follow-up permission, deadlines, and failure behavior.

Before live value, the service needs approved payment and program policies,
external reconciliation, capture-after-cancellation recovery, refund retries,
maturity controls, reproducible annual snapshots, and failed-payout handling.
The first distribution should be demonstrable end to end with advance collection,
fixed-period retries, a nonrenewing or closed account, duplicate processor events,
and lost-response recovery for one lump-sum payment. Rebate/excess examples must
not produce separate operations or completion states. Privacy
tests must cover verification, activity evidence, payment records, and backups as
well as messages.

SMTP and independent-provider federation are later operational gates. Each gate
needs measurable exit evidence appropriate to its risks; a working communications
pilot is not evidence that live financial operations or cross-provider pool
governance are complete.
