\begin{abstract}
This document describes nonnormative choices for deploying the \textit{cs-mail}
protocol. It separates the protocol's invariant meaning from operator policy:
identity enrollment, account funding, risk controls, SMTP migration, provider
clearing, privacy operations, and staged launch. The profile gives one plausible
path for an initial centralized C-SQD service and later federation. It neither
changes the settlement rules in the \textit{cs-mail Protocol Specification} nor
requires another provider to adopt the same vendors, verification evidence,
storage topology, or banking rails.
\end{abstract}

\section{Status and Document Boundary}

The \textit{cs-mail Protocol Specification} is normative. It defines permission,
bonds, repeated-attempt controls, canonical ordering, settlement, privacy, and
invariants. The \textit{Rust Reference Architecture} explains one implementation
shape. This profile addresses choices that a real operator must make but that do
not belong in the portable protocol.

In particular, this profile does not define a universal legal identity, mandate
bank-based enrollment, select a payment processor, create an additional protocol
fee, or weaken native end-to-end content encryption. Policies in this document
must remain versioned, disclosed, auditable, and subordinate to protocol
semantics.

\section{Initial Deployment Shape}

An initial deployment may place several logical roles under C-SQD while keeping
their authority and data access separate. C-SQD may operate decision ingress,
the canonical journal, relationship state, the internal ledger, native delivery,
and internal clearing. External regulated providers may fund and redeem user
balances.

Central operation does not justify a universal database credential or identifier.
Before meaningful user data accumulates, the deployment should establish separate
access domains for:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item principal evidence and account recovery;
    \item public identities and directed relationships;
    \item encrypted message content;
    \item balances, bonds, reserves, and settlement;
    \item delivery and abuse telemetry; and
    \item separately required compliance or dispute records.
\end{itemize}

Each domain should have purpose-specific credentials, encryption keys, retention
classes, and access audit. Translation among scoped references is an explicit
operation. A central operator should not treat organizational ownership as a
substitute for technical compartmentalization.

\section{Identity Enrollment and Principal Policy}

The protocol requires a continuing private principal for control, recovery, and
repeated-attempt continuity. It does not require that every provider define that
principal as a legally verified natural person. C-SQD should publish which kinds
of principal it supports, what evidence each tier requires, and which functions
depend on verification.

One deployment might accept several enrollment profiles:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.22\textwidth}YY@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Profile} & \textbf{Possible evidence} & \textbf{Policy considerations} \\
\midrule
Individual & Account authentication, recovery factors, and, where justified, legal name, date of birth, or a verified funding account. & Avoid collecting legal identity merely because it is conventional; document necessity and retention for each field. \\
Organization & Organization name, jurisdiction or registry reference, institutional funding account, and evidence that an enrolling representative may act for it. & Support delegated administration, representative turnover, and recovery without making registry data part of ordinary messages. \\
Managed group & Sponsoring organization, administrator authority, and member or role controls. & Define who owns protocol identities, receives funds, and may recover or revoke operational keys. \\
Limited account & Provider-local account and strong recovery evidence without conventional bank or legal-identity verification. & Apply proportionate balance, withdrawal, or sending limits without exposing a public identity tier. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Exact evidence is a legal, risk, accessibility, and inclusion decision. People who
cannot or should not use conventional bank-based verification need a documented
path where feasible. Identity evidence and the provider-local principal reference
must never appear in ordinary message envelopes, relationship records exposed to
other users, or globally comparable federation assertions.

Provider migration should use recipient-scoped continuity proofs for repeated-
attempt enforcement. It should not export a stable universal principal identifier.

\section{Funding, Withdrawal, and Financial Risk}

Users may fund ledger balances periodically through cards, bank transfers, or
other supported rails. Protocol settlement then occurs internally. A valid bond
must remain fully backed even if an external provider later delays, reverses, or
disputes a funding transaction.

The operator therefore needs explicit policy for:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item funding finality and any waiting period before deposited value may back
          a bond;
    \item withdrawal availability for returned collateral and received
          settlement;
    \item chargebacks, fraud losses, negative balances, account suspension, and
          separately authorized compensating ledger entries;
    \item limits by verification tier, settlement unit, jurisdiction, or risk;
    \item sanctions, tax, money-transmission, consumer-protection, and abandoned-
          property obligations where applicable; and
    \item dispute windows and retention classes for evidence that must survive
          longer than ordinary delivery data.
\end{itemize}

External reversals do not rewrite a terminal protocol event. If policy authorizes
a later adjustment, the ledger records a new compensating batch with its own
cause, authority, and audit trail.

The sender-facing interface should distinguish available, reserved, pending-
release, and withdrawable value. Before authorization it should show the full
$C+S+L_k$ requirement, the short admission window, the decision-window duration
that begins only after admission, the earliest final expiry time, and the
persistence-reserve release condition. A still-unadmitted reservation should
offer cancellation and explain that cancellation or admission timeout returns all
reserved value.

\section{SMTP Migration}

SMTP is an edge compatibility mechanism, not the native trust or privacy model.
The gateway should make the route and its privacy consequences visible.

\subsection{Inbound Legacy Sender}

When a conventional sender contacts a C-SQD recipient, the gateway can follow
this sequence:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item Resolve whether the legacy sender maps to an accepted protocol identity.
    \item If accepted-sender authority exists, apply ordinary accepted-sender and
          safety policy.
    \item Otherwise, return a standards-compatible challenge or a web link that
          explains the recipient's current contact terms.
    \item Authenticate or enroll the sender, present the complete reservation,
          and fund the sender account if necessary.
    \item Obtain fresh signed terms and reserve the bond and any persistence
          reserve with a short admission deadline.
    \item Durably record the encrypted content and retryable delivery intent, then
          admit the attempt through the same deterministic protocol command used
          by a native client. If this does not happen before the admission
          deadline, return $C+S+L_k$ in full.
    \item Return an authenticated admission result without implying inbox rank,
          recipient attention, or a guaranteed reply.
\end{enumerate}

The web flow must bind the funded authorization to the exact sender, recipient,
message, quote, amount, admission deadline, and later decision deadline. A
reusable payment link or bearer token must not let another party substitute
content or redirect a reservation.

A legacy gateway may encounter plaintext before creating native ciphertext. The
message must therefore be marked as outside the native endpoint-to-endpoint
confidentiality guarantee. Transport TLS and provider encryption at rest are not
equivalent to native end-to-end encryption.

\subsection{Outbound Legacy Recipient}

When a native user sends to a conventional recipient, the client should disclose
that the destination cannot enforce native permission, settlement, graph privacy,
or content confidentiality. The envelope and local history should record the
downgrade. The system must not advertise a successful SMTP delivery as recipient
acceptance of a \textit{cs-mail} relationship.

\subsection{Gateway Abuse Controls}

A payment challenge is itself a communication surface. Gateways should rate-limit
challenges, prevent address enumeration, avoid leaking recipient pricing to
unauthorized bulk probes, expire unfunded flows, and provide a way to report
impersonation or malicious content. Gateway policy may reject malware, unlawful
content, or excessive volume independently of financial validity.

\section{Provider Clearing}

Federation creates obligations between providers when recipient providers earn
$C$ or recipients receive $S$. The persistence reserve $L_k$ remains sender
property and never enters provider revenue or inter-provider clearing.

Providers may preserve each authenticated protocol event while periodically
netting external payment. Suppose Provider A owes Provider B $10{,}000$ during a
clearing period and Provider B owes Provider A $8{,}000$. The external rail may
settle a $2{,}000$ net obligation from A to B. The underlying events remain
individually attributable through scoped settlement receipts so participants can
verify their balances and authorized disputes can be resolved.

A clearing agreement should define:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item supported settlement units and no implicit foreign-exchange rule;
    \item clearing intervals, collateral or credit limits, and default handling;
    \item canonical cutoffs and treatment of late or disputed events;
    \item reconciliation proofs and scoped audit references;
    \item who bears external-rail fees without changing quoted protocol
          settlement; and
    \item suspension and orderly wind-down without stranding participant-owned
          balances or pending protocol decisions.
\end{itemize}

Netting changes how providers discharge obligations. It does not change who owns
value after any acceptance, rejection, expiry, or unadmitted-cancellation
transition.

\section{Operational Policy and User Experience}

The recipient and recipient provider have economic incentives that require
visible counter-controls. C-SQD should publish bounds or review procedures for
$C$ and provider minimum collateral, prohibit interfaces that hide timely
acceptance, issue signed decision receipts, detect recipient-provider collusion,
and provide dispute and suspension procedures.

The product should teach relationship consequences at the decision point. An
accept, reject, or block control beside a message should state that the action
applies to the directed sender identity, settles every decision-open admitted bond
for that relationship, and fully cancels its unadmitted reservations. Acceptance
releases persistence reserves only for attempts from that public identity; it
does not reset principal-level backoff or release reserves belonging to another
identity of the same principal. Rejection declines the active solicitation while
preserving a later eligible retry after backoff. Blocking is the separate control
for prohibiting future ordinary contact until the recipient removes the block. A
late acceptance should distinguish prospective permission from older bonds whose
decision windows are already closed.

The sender-facing flow should distinguish reservation from admission. Closing the
client, cancelling, or failing to make encrypted content and its delivery intent
durable before the admission deadline returns $C+S+L_k$ in full, advances no
backoff, and produces no recipient solicitation. The recipient decision window
starts only at canonical admission.

The first admitted attempt for an unaccepted directed relationship should open one
grouped relationship solicitation. That group is the one logical establishment
prompt; recipient settings may present it as a push, badge, banner, inbox row, or
quiet equivalent. Later admitted messages join that active group.
They may update its contents or ordinary unread count quietly, but must not
repeatedly ask the recipient to establish the same relationship. This behavior is
solicitation coalescing, not message idempotency: every attempt retains its own
bond, deadline, content, and repeated-attempt consequence.

Accept, reject, and block close the active solicitation and apply to the
relationship. Reject permits a later eligible attempt after the applicable
backoff. Block refuses future terms, reservations, any optional capability use,
and admission until the recipient unblocks; unblocking does not accept the sender or erase
backoff. Ignoring the solicitation means taking no action, so bonds still expire
individually. Dismissing or hiding the prompt is a presentation preference and
must not be represented as acceptance, rejection, blocking, or financial
settlement. Once the final associated bond is no longer decision-open, the episode
may lapse. A later eligible attempt may open one new episode after the applicable
backoff unless the relationship is blocked.

Operational dashboards should report service health without reconstructing a
global relationship graph. Metrics should use short windows, aggregation, scoped
trace references, and documented retention. Support access to identity,
relationship, content, or ledger data should be separately authorized rather than
granted through one universal administrator role.

\section{Staged Launch}

Express lanes are a launch extension outside settlement state. Their recipient-
signed grants authorize only rate- and time-bounded bond-free communication, open
no relationship solicitation, advance no repeated-attempt state, and remain
subordinate to recipient blocking.

A cautious deployment can proceed in five gates:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item \textbf{Closed deterministic kernel.} Execute every transition and
          invariant in memory using fixed policy and explicit canonical time.
    \item \textbf{Internal-value pilot.} Add the append-only ledger, transactional
          outbox, crash recovery, and synthetic or nonwithdrawable test value.
    \item \textbf{Native-client pilot.} Add endpoint encryption, key transparency,
          recovery, privacy-domain credentials, retention, and verified deletion.
    \item \textbf{Limited external funding and SMTP.} Introduce real rails and
          gateways under conservative limits, visible privacy downgrades, and
          staffed dispute operations.
    \item \textbf{Federation.} Add independent providers only after golden
          transition vectors, authenticated receipts, clearing reconciliation,
          migration proofs, and adversarial failure tests are stable.
\end{enumerate}

Each gate should have measurable exit criteria. Volume growth is not evidence
that value conservation, privacy deletion, decision timeliness, or dispute
operations work correctly.

\section{Conclusion}

The first C-SQD deployment can be centralized without treating deployment policy
as protocol law. Identity evidence, funding rails, SMTP behavior, risk controls,
and clearing agreements should remain explicit profiles around the deterministic
kernel. Preserving that boundary allows the product to evolve, jurisdictions to
differ, and later providers to interoperate without changing the meaning of
permission or settlement.
