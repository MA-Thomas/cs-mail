\begin{abstract}
This architecture follows one relationship request from charge preparation to
recipient decision, then follows a forfeiture into the annual member program and
one automatic bank payment of the member's allocation.
A pure transition computes the required state and accounting obligations. A
transaction commits them with an outbox; the configured processor confirms payment
effects afterward. This division gives the revised protocol a small deterministic
center while keeping delivery, payment uncertainty, and member eligibility in
explicitly bounded services.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Status and reading order.}
The \textit{Protocol Specification}, normative financial-program profile, and
\href{DOMAIN_MODEL.md}{C-SQD domain model} control the rules. This document
describes the Rust libraries and durable boundaries; a running product server,
desktop application, and live payment adapter remain product work. Request
classes and atomic lane acceptance are implemented, including native lane sends.
The implementation notes describe the coordinated format cutover.

\section{One Request, Three Messages, One Decision}

Alice selects Bob's research class; signed terms fix the class, $C+S$, and policy
versions. Creation records one payment intent. After finalized funding, submission
commits encrypted content, delivery intent, a deadline, and one solicitation.
The directed pair has one nonterminal request across devices and classes.

Bob permits two follow-ups. Each has its own ciphertext, authenticated declarations,
and delivery state; allowance is consumed, but charge, history, and deadline do not change.

Standing or lane acceptance atomically commits its permission, one $C+S$ refund
obligation, request closure, and expiry cancellation. Alice may use the permission
while refund execution awaits processor confirmation.

\section{The Pure Transition and Its Durable Boundary}

\subsection{Authority enters as evidence}

Wire decoding first checks versions, sizes, and canonical structure. Signature
verification then binds the exact command digest to an authorized actor, key,
deployment, provider, and receipt. Finally, domain conversion checks scope and
command-specific constraints. An authenticated wrapper should have private
constructors; a public boolean claiming authentication cannot carry this meaning.

The recipient's device time is not authoritative. Ingress durably records
authenticated receipt before returning evidence to the client. That record lets a
timely acceptance beat an expiry worker even when decision processing is delayed.

\subsection{A snapshot contains the facts that can change}

For acceptance, the snapshot contains the relationship, its current request if
any, request-history context, relevant payment/refund facts, and ledger references.
It carries versions and enough completeness evidence to distinguish no current
request from a missing projection. Historical pending refunds may still exist
after a request closes; they remain separate obligations rather than additional
open relationship requests.
Bounded acceptance also requires the authorized lane grant and protected capability state.

The pure function has a compact shape:
\begin{lstlisting}
pub fn transition(
    snapshot: &SettlementSnapshot,
    authorized: &KernelCommand<ProtocolCommand>,
    context: &TransitionContext,
) -> Result<TransitionManifest, ProtocolError>;
\end{lstlisting}
It performs no I/O and reads no implicit clock or live policy service. Time,
versions, immutable terms, and authenticated evidence are inputs. Its result
describes the complete consequence, not just a new relationship-state enum.

\subsection{The manifest describes the complete consequence}

\texttt{TransitionManifest} contains expected snapshot and ledger revisions;
the next protocol state, request history, payment records, and messages;
a ledger batch; schedule changes; protocol events; outbox intents; and any terms
result. Forfeitures and holds are explicit outputs as well. This lets one
transition describe both permission and the money that follows from it.

An acceptance includes its required request outcome and refund obligation.
The extension must coordinate the lane grant in that same commit for bounded
acceptance, without changing the relationship to standing acceptance.
A rejection records pending forfeiture without crediting the recipient.
Follow-up admission cannot introduce another charge. The manifest's fields are
public Rust data; correctness comes from the transition, validated commit path,
and conformance tests, rather than a claim that private constructors alone
prevent every inconsistent value.

Storage checks revisions and commits the manifest atomically. The PostgreSQL
implementation uses transaction locks and version checks around the affected
records. Application controllers do not recompute financial consequences after
the kernel returns. Transactional outbox records make post-commit delivery and
payment work recoverable.

\section{Objects That Keep Different Lifecycles Apart}

The relationship persists across requests. A request starts in
\texttt{PreparingSubmission}. Its \texttt{SubmissionPreparation} records the
deadline for completing funding and preparing the initial message.
\texttt{CancelRequestSubmission} can cancel it during this phase.

Once those requirements are satisfied, \texttt{SubmitRequestToRecipient} commits
the initial delivery. The lifecycle becomes \texttt{AwaitingRecipientDecision};
its \texttt{RequestSubmission} records submission time and decision deadline.
Acceptance, rejection, and expiry close the submitted request. In the extension,
an accepted request identifies standing permission or a lane; the latter concerns
the relationship and is usually not scoped to one conversation. These names
identify what is being prepared and whose decision is pending. ``Admission''
remains the broader per-message permission check.

A payment operation has a separate lifecycle. Pending external evidence is not
finalized funding, and a refund can remain unpaid after its request is terminal.
An annual allocation likewise remains owed after service coverage ends.
\texttt{AnnualDistributionId} identifies the network allocation year;
\texttt{BillingAccountId} identifies the person's bank-linked account;
\texttt{ServiceContractId} identifies a fixed service purchase and is distinct
from the \texttt{PaymentOperationId} of a collection attempt.
Neither is a public communication identity or a private anti-abuse principal.

Money uses integer minor units and an explicit settlement unit. Checked
arithmetic rejects overflow and mixed-unit operations. Corporate rates use exact
basis-point arithmetic. Submission deadlines, decision deadlines, message
validity, annual allocation cutoffs, service intervals, and payment dates answer
different questions and remain explicit inputs.

Quoted financial terms stay fixed for a request. The operator's
\texttt{RequestPricingPolicy} bounds collateral and sets the processing charge.
\texttt{RecipientRequestClasses} checks at most eight user-defined classes and
unique IDs, including on deserialization. The application authenticates the
recipient's publication under protected current authority. The resolver returns
checked \texttt{ResolvedRequestPricing}; signed terms retain a
\texttt{SelectedRequestClass} snapshot. There is no fallback to caller-supplied
prices or an invented default class. Class edits do not rewrite existing quotes
or partition principal-recipient history. Current follow-up policy is checked
separately for each message.

\section{Payment Work Is a Recoverable Conversation}

\subsection{Create before dispatch}

Creation claims the unique nonterminal request for the directed pair and records
one authorized payment intent before calling a provider. A database uniqueness
constraint or equivalent aggregate guard prevents two devices with different
keys from dispatching two charges for the same pending relationship request.
The worker's external idempotency key derives from that committed operation,
not from a fresh network retry.

An external timeout means uncertainty. The worker queries the original operation
or repeats it under the provider's idempotency contract; it cannot assume failure
and invent a replacement. Submission waits for finalized funding evidence and durable content. The platform does not rely on an authorization
remaining open throughout the decision window.

\subsection{New funding attempts require separate authority}

\texttt{PaymentOutcome::Pending} records uncertainty;
\texttt{PaymentOutcome::Settled} represents authenticated satisfaction of the
provider's funding-finality policy. A real adapter must map rail-specific evidence
to that contract. Authorization or receipt of a provider request alone is
insufficient. The current simulator can delay resolution, fail an operation,
lose a response, or report a later reversal.

\texttt{FundingSource} in \texttt{cs-mail-finance} owns verification, restriction,
and attempt accounting. PostgreSQL shares the source record across an account's
request and utility charges. Reserving a new operation consumes available attempt
capacity; reconciling the same uncertain operation does not. Failure or reversal
restricts the source. \texttt{maximum\_unresolved} bounds simultaneous unresolved
attempts, not lifetime attempts. Authenticated bank evidence permits
re-verification of the same person and route; bank replacement remains deferred.
An explicit account command can request a collection retry while retaining the
contract's terms. A worker cannot manufacture authority with another operation ID.

A reservation is not a dispatch grant. The PostgreSQL
\texttt{authorize\_request\_dispatch} and \texttt{authorize\_utility\_dispatch}
transactions check current source restrictions before durably granting a new
dispatch. Previously authorized work remains reconcilable under its operation
identity. Restricted queued work waits for re-verification. A Rust value created
earlier cannot prove that mutable external restrictions have remained unchanged.

\subsection{Cancellation can precede confirmation}

If submission timeout or sender cancellation commits first, the request stays
cancelled. The payment adapter voids an uncaptured authorization or refunds a
capture. If a capture confirmation arrives afterward, the reconciler records the
financial fact and schedules the full refund without reopening the request.
This recovery completes the existing obligation after the request has closed.

\texttt{PaymentProcessor} exposes submission, lookup, and explicit
\texttt{cancel\_capture} with a validated \texttt{CaptureCancellation}.
Cancellation reaches a pending capture rather than being consumed by lookup.
\texttt{SimulatedProcessor} exercises these contracts; one configured processor
authority serves the deployment. This trait is not a multi-processor router.

Provider notifications are authenticated evidence. Deduplicate them by provider
event and economic operation, check amount, unit, and destination scope, and
reconcile out-of-order facts without regressing confirmed state. Both polling
and webhooks may discover the same event; neither may duplicate a refund.

\subsection{Completion is separate from obligation}

Refund and payout workers read durable obligations and execute stable operations.
Failure leaves an obligation pending or under review. Success records reconciled
confirmation and the corresponding ledger effect. A chargeback or adjudicated
correction appends a separately authorized batch. It does not overwrite a
recipient decision or confiscate a finalized member allocation.

The ledger therefore projects utility-service receipts, request charges, refunds due and completed,
pending forfeitures, corporate revenue, restricted member funds, and payables.
It does not expose a user wallet. A refund or payable cannot be applied as
available funds for another request.

\section{From a Rejection to an Annual Allocation}

Bob's rejection records processing revenue $C$ and a pending forfeiture lot for
$S$. The lot retains the request's quoted rate version. Maturity evaluation is a
separate decision over verified financial facts and policy; a calendar boundary
does not make unresolved funds distributable.

\texttt{cs-mail-finance} owns the financial program. An
\texttt{AnnualDistributionSchedule} fixes a UTC calendar year and eligibility
policy. At finalization, \texttt{FinancialProgram} fixes qualifying membership
and eligible funding, assesses each new lot once, and records corporate and member
amounts, equal individual payables, and restricted remainder. It consumes no
renewal or billing status when deciding eligibility. The deployment profile
defines the arithmetic; this domain supplies its single implementation.

The current PostgreSQL finalizer loads the program records and ledger under a
transaction lock and commits the allocation as one outcome. Replay reuses the
finalized result. It does not implement chunked preparation for large membership
sets. A future bounded-batch design must preserve the same atomic visibility and
once-only assessment guarantees.

A \texttt{MemberPayable} belongs to one member and pool year. Failed or delayed
payments stay assigned, outside the next year's equal split. Its private amount
and published \texttt{DistributionTerms} determine reporting values
$R_i=\min(D_i,U)$ and $X_i=\max(0,D_i-U)$. Those terms snapshot the annual
utility amount and due date independently of renewal. The payable owns at most
one \texttt{PaymentExecution}, never separate rebate and cash executions.
Authorized corrections append records with a stated cause; they do not rewrite
the annual snapshot or create another ordinary annual allocation.

\section{Billing Owns Fixed Service Purchases and Coverage}

\texttt{cs-mail-billing} owns the bank-linked \texttt{BillingAccount}.
It publishes \texttt{ServiceOffer} values and records each purchase as a
\texttt{ServiceContract}.
A validated \texttt{ServicePeriod} spans one calendar/anniversary year with an
explicit leap-day rule. An offer pins its policy version, price, settlement unit,
collection date, and interval. Its constructor requires collection before the
interval starts; the product requires collection well in advance, with the exact
lead time supplied by policy. No universal date or number of days is invented.

Purchase snapshots those terms into a contract. Collection retries preserve
price and interval; they never start a fresh year from the retry date. Coverage
requires finalized funding, the current time within that fixed interval, and
funding already completed at that time. Late funding grants no past service.
Distribution does not alter the contract or revoke coverage.

\texttt{BankVerification} binds person, member, account, bank token, scope, unit,
and verification version under the configured bank authority. Verification
produces an opaque \texttt{VerifiedBankAccount}, not a deserializable claim of
verification. Trusted registration enforces one person/account, member and alias
ownership, and explicit account actor grants. Bank replacement and broader
account-management UX remain deferred; this boundary is not a live bank connector.

\texttt{SignedBillingCommand} binds account, scope, revision, idempotency, and
command to an active operational key under a distinct signing context. Explicit
account grants authorize inspection, purchase of a published offer, collection
retry, and closure. These commands cannot choose a processor, set policy prices,
or supply payout instructions. There is no independent user-managed billing key.
Closure stops coverage but preserves the bank association needed for obligations.

\section{The Application Prepares One Automatic Distribution}

Finance owns eligibility, pool-year allocation, published distribution terms,
and the resulting obligation. Billing supplies the verified bank association.
The pure coordinator, \texttt{prepare\_distribution}, checks that association,
scope, unit, payable, and due time, then prepares one bank payout of
the full allocation. It does not examine renewal or available refund capacity.
Pending next-year collection and account closure do not gate this payment.

\texttt{PaymentExecution} retains sequential attempts and authenticated events.
Its states distinguish pending, settled, failed, voided, and reversed, with the
required evidence carried by each state. A lost response reuses the operation
identity. Only a definite failed or voided attempt permits a replacement attempt;
conflicting evidence is rejected. Reversal retains settlement and reversal
evidence and the unpaid obligation; it is not silently reissued.

PostgreSQL uses the same coordinator, commits payable changes and work together,
and loads the associated bank route rather than user-supplied distribution
instructions. Annual finalization atomically queues automatic preparation;
workers wait for the published due time. Preparing one member's payment uses
selected owner records rather than loading the population. Complete-program
operations, such as annual finalization, reject such incomplete views.

Service collection and distribution remain separate transactions and worker
operations. One distribution has one completion status; rebate and excess have
no independent retries. Statements derive the two reporting amounts from the
fixed allocation and utility amount, including after closure or nonrenewal.

\section{Admission, Scheduling, and Concurrency}

The host checks funded local service coverage before new communication. This
applies to the local recipient and any local sender, including accepted and lane
paths; a remote sender's provider owns its coverage check. Coverage expiry leaves
recipient decisions, financial recovery, and finalized allocations available.

All delivery paths observe blocking under the directed-pair transaction boundary.
Standing acceptance and valid lanes authorize delivery without a request charge.
An authenticated follow-up additionally identifies a request awaiting a decision and consumes
the recipient's current allowance. Only eligible new-request creation may start
a new payment operation, and it requires explicit sender authorization.

Follow-up policy updates, request closure, and delivery admission serialize.
Content and a retryable delivery effect must be durable before admission commits.
Message validity is checked independently of the request's common deadline.
Lanes retain their signed scope and hard validity limits. Bounded acceptance must
commit lane authority with request settlement; later lane changes do not reopen
the outcome or create member activity. Independent issuance during a pending
request and bounded-acceptance timing edge cases remain open in the domain model.

Scheduled work includes submission timeout, request expiry, payment
reconciliation, annual allocation, member payment preparation, and retention.
Maturity evidence must be recorded through the financial program before allocation.
Each job carries a target, earliest execution time, version/evidence preconditions,
and stable operation identity. Leased workers submit commands against current
state. They do not assume that a row remains unchanged because a job was queued.

Principal-scoped new-request history needs its own concurrency guard shared by
aliases. It advances only at initial submission. A follow-up, cancelled preparation,
key rotation, or class switching cannot bypass that distinction. Existing late
standing acceptance changes future
permission while old expiry processing preserves that permission and materializes
only the old request's financial obligation.

\section{Responsibility Boundaries in the Workspace}

The dependency direction follows ownership. Primitives provide money, time, and
scoped identifiers. The ledger validates balanced batches. Finance depends on
those foundations for payment evidence, request finances, forfeiture maturity,
member eligibility, annual allocation, and payables. Billing builds on financial
operations to own fixed service purchases and coverage. It does not decide who
qualifies for a share.

Protocol owns request and relationship transitions and request pricing.
Capabilities own lane grants and allowance. Security verifies communication
and pricing authority; billing commands have their own signing contract. The
application layer coordinates complete use cases, including the billing/finance
automatic distribution, without acquiring either domain's policy decisions.
For bounded acceptance, capabilities validate the grant, protocol determines
settlement, and application coordination commits both atomically. A grant also
resolves a pending request when issued separately: preparation is cancelled with
void/full refund, and a submitted request still pending is accepted even after its
deadline. Terminal settlements stay fixed; future access follows the lane.

PostgreSQL owns durable transactions, ordering, uniqueness, journals, and outbox
work. Workers lease that work, call domain operations through the application or
storage boundary, and reconcile external evidence. Payment adapters execute
operations and translate provider evidence; they cannot set eligibility, invent
new funding authority, or change request outcomes.

Content and wire crates retain encryption and canonical declaration binding.
The service exposes authenticated operations, and the client signs commands and
consumes outcomes. Product interfaces build on these APIs rather than reconstruct
settlement from presentation state. API, wire, event, protocol, database, and
financial-policy versions remain distinct. Separate processes or credentials may
be needed for privacy and operational isolation; a crate boundary alone supplies
neither.

\section{Privacy Extends to the Member Program}

No routine component needs the entire story. The identity service knows control
and recovery evidence. The relationship service sees scoped permission and request
state. Content storage sees ciphertext. The ledger sees scoped financial causes
and amounts. Membership verification sees the evidence needed to establish one
qualifying member, while the allocation engine can consume a minimal eligibility
assertion. Telemetry receives short-lived, rotated references.

Use separate access roles, keys, and credentials, including for financial and
membership records that must outlive ordinary delivery data. Public identities
and private principals must not become universal ledger or telemetry join keys.
Native content is encrypted at endpoints; a payment or membership check does
not authorize provider decryption. Authenticated purpose and origin remain
accountable declarations, not proof of plaintext truth.

Eligibility activity should record only the minimal facts required by policy.
Background synchronization and inbound lane traffic are not substitutes for
intentional use. The payment processor receives required transaction references
and amounts, not message content or the user's unrelated relationships.

Retention classes define purpose, key scope, operational lifetime, dispute or
required retention, and deletion method. A settled request can lose unnecessary
message linkage while an unpaid refund or member payable retains enough evidence
for payment and audit. Continuing permission does not justify retaining its
formation history forever. Cryptographic erasure must cover wrapped keys,
replicas, backups, logs, and derived indexes; separately retained records must
not be described as erased or two-party-only archives.

Logs and errors use safe event types and redacted identifiers. They must contain
no plaintext, secrets, complete financial snapshots, or private identity evidence.
Pairwise scoping reduces direct linkage but does not eliminate timing or amount
correlation, so retention and access discipline remain necessary.

\section{Version Boundaries and Evidence of Correctness}

The clean domain update directly replaces the old billing cycles, split payments,
and distribution instructions. It has no compatibility aliases or fallback
readers. Wire format is 6, stored protocol format is 8, and stored financial-program
format is 6. The revised development schema requires fresh initialization;
populated older records are not converted by the migrations.

Private domain fields and checked constructors prevent invalid combinations in
normal use. Stored rehydration additionally validates contracts, executions,
payables, funding sources, request finances, and program records against their
scope, ownership, arithmetic, evidence, and ledger. Durable uniqueness, dispatch
checks, idempotency, and worker fencing address facts that local Rust types alone
cannot guarantee.

The first story test uses one charged request and three permitted messages.
Acceptance creates one refund obligation, and provider confirmation completes it
once. Variants reject into pending forfeiture, expire into a collateral refund,
disable follow-ups, and race policy changes with admission. Other tests exercise
two-device creation, alias history, cancellation followed by late capture,
duplicate/out-of-order notifications, and unknown commit recovery.
Existing scenarios now exercise fixed class terms, publication limits, lane/refund
rollback and retry, preparation and late grants, and native lane allowance. Shared
history and terminal settlement remain domain invariants; passing scenarios are
limited evidence, not exhaustive proof.

Financial-program tests reproduce a year from fixed input manifests, exclude
refundable and immature funds, assess each eligible lot once, preserve quoted
rate cohorts, and allocate equal integer shares. Include zero members, small
amounts, unallocated remainder, failed payments, and
annual finalization retries after a crash. A later year must not assess old member funds
or redistribute already assigned obligations. Billing tests cover advance
collection, fixed-period retries, leap-day rules, pending funding, and coverage
boundaries. Domain and PostgreSQL acceptance stories cover one verified
person/account, explicit actor grants, current restrictions on queued work,
automatic payment after closure with next-year funding pending, and lost-response
recovery for one lump sum. With $U=\$120$, \$80/\$120/\$170 allocation
examples verify reporting without separate payment components. Corrupt evidence,
malformed stored owners, and incomplete program views are rejected.

Property and concurrency tests check conservation, finality, uniqueness, scope,
and recovery at durable boundaries. Privacy tests exercise cross-domain credential
denial, captured-log redaction, and deletion through backup restoration. Fuzz
canonical decoding, signatures, and content bindings.

Independent-provider federation follows only after a financial agreement assigns
pool governance, membership uniqueness, refund and payout obligations, clearing,
and losses. The local manifest remains useful as a reference outcome, but the
old direct-recipient clearing model is not carried forward automatically.
