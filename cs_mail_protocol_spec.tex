\begin{abstract}
The \textit{cs-mail} protocol makes permission to communicate a directed
relationship. A sender who lacks permission may submit a relationship request
under a recipient-defined class, with an initial encrypted message and one
conditional charge. The recipient may accept with standing permission or an
express lane, reject, or leave the request to expire. These outcomes determine refunds
and forfeitures; permitted follow-up messages create no further charge. This
specification defines the request lifecycle, authenticated decisions, financial
obligations, and privacy boundaries. The C-SQD deployment adds a company-owned
pool and annual equal member allocations under its financial-program profile.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reading the Protocol Through One Request}

Alice wants to propose research collaboration to Professor Bob. Bob has not accepted her
public identity, and no scoped capability authorizes her message. Alice obtains
terms for his published research request class: processing component $C=0.50$ and refundable
collateral component $S=5.00$. She authorizes a charge of $5.50$ associated with
that request. Submission occurs once funding is finalized, encrypted content is
durable, and the service can commit a retryable delivery intent.

Bob sees one relationship request. If his settings permit follow-ups, Alice may
add messages to it without another charge or a later decision deadline. Accepting
the request grants either standing directed permission or a relationship-level
express lane and records a $5.50$ refund obligation. Rejecting
her retains $0.50$ as processing revenue and records $5.00$ as pending forfeiture.
Bob does not acquire a personal claim on that collateral. If the request expires
without a timely decision, the processing amount is retained and $5.00$ is due
back to Alice.

Those are protocol outcomes. The external provider may confirm a refund later.
Likewise, a forfeiture does not become immediately available for distribution:
the C-SQD profile separates unresolved exposure, eligible forfeitures, the
corporate allocation, restricted member funds, and finalized member payables.
This separation explains the objects and transitions that follow.

\section{Status, Scope, and Conformance}

This specification defines the request and permission contract. The Rust reference
architecture identifies its implementation boundaries; the desktop plan describes
the remaining product work. Conformance to the contract does not by itself
establish readiness for live financial operations. The words \MUST, \MUSTNOT, \SHOULD,
\SHOULDNOT, and \MAY{} describe conformance to this draft.
Request classes and lane acceptance are implemented by the Rust libraries.
Signed quote requests select a recipient-defined class; the signed lane-grant
API coordinates permission and request settlement. Product transports remain
deployment work.

The protocol governs directed permission, one charge per request, request
outcomes, financial obligations, authenticated admission, replay, and privacy.
The section titled \emph{C-SQD Financial Program} in the \textit{Deployment and
Migration Profile} is normative for a deployment advertising that profile. It
defines the company-owned pool and equal annual distribution rules. Parameters
explicitly marked proposed there are not launch commitments. A live deployment
\MUST{} publish a complete, versioned policy before accepting real charges.

This document does not choose a banking partner, merchant arrangement, wire
encoding, cryptographic suite, or jurisdiction-specific legal classification.
The charge/refund structure and outsourcing of payments do not by themselves
establish a regulatory exemption.

An implementation \MUST{} identify the protocol and financial-policy versions
used for every request. Old quotes and obligations \MUSTNOT{} be silently
reinterpreted under this draft. Migration requires explicit treatment of existing
requests, refunds, and entitlements; a changed document is not a migration event.

\section{Permission, Requests, and Messages}

\subsection{The identities involved}

A public protocol identity $I_i$ is the addressable persona another participant
can accept or block. Its controlling provider maintains authorized operational
keys, status, and version. A provider-local private principal $P_i$ attaches
control, recovery, and anti-abuse continuity to one or more public identities.
The general protocol can represent people or organizational principals; that
scope does not establish organizational accounts in the current C-SQD product.
C-SQD currently binds one identifiable person to one account, with multiple
email addresses permitted and identity tied to a verified bank account.
A principal is not automatically an enrolled distribution-program member.
The \href{DOMAIN_MODEL.md}{C-SQD domain model} and financial deployment profile
define these product rules; distinct scoped identifiers retain their privacy roles.

Permission is directed from $I_i$ to $I_j$. Accepting one identity \MUSTNOT{}
accept another identity controlled by the same principal. Conversely, rotating
identities, keys, devices, or providers \MUSTNOT{} erase principal-scoped
request history for the same recipient. Outside its identity authority, the
principal \MUST{} be represented by purpose-limited scoped assertions, never a
globally comparable identifier.

\subsection{The enduring relationship}

The relationship has state
\[
A_{ij}\in\{\texttt{unknown},\texttt{accepted},\texttt{expressLane},\texttt{rejected},
\texttt{revoked},\texttt{blocked}\}.
\]
Its record contains a scoped directed-pair handle, monotonically increasing
version, latest canonical event position, and transition time. Accepted ordinary
communication requires no request charge. A valid scoped capability can authorize
narrower communication without establishing acceptance. Blocking overrides both.
Acceptance of a \emph{request} with a lane does not set the relationship to
\texttt{accepted}; it records \texttt{expressLane} with its lane identifier.
Request classes describe proposed approaches, not enduring
relationship types or automatic permission grants.

Unknown, rejected, and revoked relationships may be eligible for a new request
under recipient and anti-abuse policy. Thus a request is needed when standing
permission and valid lane access are absent, not only when no relationship
record exists. Rejection
declines a request; blocking prohibits further ordinary contact until removed.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\subsection{The relationship request}

A request is the economic unit. It \MUST{} have a unique scoped identifier,
directed relationship and generation, selected recipient-defined request class,
immutable quote, initial-message binding,
original charge reference, creation time, submission deadline, optional submission
time and decision deadline, state, and version. The request lifecycle has two
nonterminal states. Submission commits the initial communication for delivery;
message admission is the more general check of whether a particular message
may proceed under a request, relationship, or capability. The states are:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em,style=nextline]
\item[\texttt{PreparingSubmission}] The request exists, but its initial
communication has not yet been committed for delivery to the recipient. Funding
and content preparation must finish before the submission deadline.
\item[\texttt{AwaitingRecipientDecision}] Submission has committed and fixed the
decision deadline. The recipient may decide, and permitted follow-ups may join
the request.
\end{description}
The terminal outcomes are \texttt{Accepted}, \texttt{Rejected},
\texttt{Expired}, and \texttt{Cancelled}.
An accepted request records whether it granted standing permission or an express
lane; both modes have the same refund consequence.

At most one nonterminal request
\MUST{} exist per directed public-identity pair. Creation and external-charge
dispatch \MUST{} enforce that constraint even across devices or different
idempotency keys. A second submission returns the existing request or a stable
conflict; it does not authorize another charge. Selecting a different request
class \MUSTNOT{} evade this constraint.

The charge purchases submission of a relationship request with an initial
communication. It does not guarantee acceptance, attention, inbox prominence,
or a response. Ordinary delivery recovery after submission does not undo the
request. A sender cannot cancel a submitted request to avoid its outcome.

\subsection{Messages while awaiting the recipient's decision}

Submission creates one logical solicitation. Later messages may join only
when authenticated recipient follow-up policy permits them. Each message retains
its own identifier, ciphertext, envelope validity, and delivery state. It does
not acquire a new financial decision window.

An allowed follow-up \MUSTNOT{} create another charge, solicitation generation,
forfeiture or new-request history increment, and
\MUSTNOT{} extend the request deadline. An exact replay returns the original
admission result without consuming allowance twice. Recipient policy may disable
follow-ups or bound count, rate, size, and declared scope. Its version and the
allowance consumed \MUST{} be recorded at admission.

Follow-up permission is admission authority. Notification style, unread counts,
ranking, and dismissing a prompt are presentation preferences and do not decide
the request. A disabled follow-up \MUST{} fail without offering an automatic
second charge against the pending request.

\section{Fixed Terms and Canonical Time}

\subsection{What the sender authorizes}

Before creating a request, the sender obtains signed terms containing the quote
identifier; protocol, policy, and financial-program versions; scoped parties and
service operator; relationship and request-history versions; the selected class
and its published meaning; $C$ and $S$;
settlement unit; submission and decision durations; request eligibility time;
quote issue and expiry times; and refund and forfeiture rules. The terms
\MUST{} include the applicable corporate-share rate or an immutable reference
to it. Any declaration-dependent pricing or eligibility \MUST{} bind a canonical
digest of the initial message's authenticated declarations.

The base charge is
\[
Q=C+S.
\]
The service operator sets $C$, a bounded menu for $S$, and a default selection.
A recipient publishes at most eight user-defined request classes and chooses collateral for each from that
menu through authenticated preferences. The sender selects from those classes. The
C-SQD defaults are specified in the deployment profile; the protocol does not
embed a particular price or currency. The full
amount and possible outcomes \MUST{} be disclosed before authorization. The
corporate share is an allocation from eligible forfeitures, not an additional
sender fee. The selected class and quoted financial terms \MUST{} remain
immutable after creation.

Recipient preferences affect new quotes only. A preference change \MUSTNOT{}
alter the amount on an existing request. The base request has no additional
persistence reserve, and follow-ups add no financial component.

\subsection{Time and receipt authority}

Every state-changing command \MUST{} have an authoritative receipt time and one
canonical linearization position. Device clocks cannot determine financial
timeliness. Authenticated recipient decisions \MUST{} be durably received before
the provider issues a signed receipt or claims they were received.

Creation materializes the submission deadline from canonical creation time.
Submission materializes one immutable decision deadline $T$ from canonical
submission time. Arithmetic \MUST{} be checked for overflow. A message's separate
validity deadline only gates that message's admission; it cannot shorten or
extend $T$, alter a submitted request's outcome, or authorize early deletion of
content still required for delivery.

A decision is timely when durably received at or before $T$. Expiry \MUSTNOT{}
become final until the journal establishes that no applicable timely decision
remains unprocessed. A bounded processing holdback may permit sequencing of
already received decisions; it does not extend the recipient's decision window.

\section{Financial Obligations and External Execution}

\subsection{One request, one charge}

The service \MUST{} associate every charge operation with the authorized request
and original payment reference. An uncertain provider result is not a failed
charge: it \MUST{} be reconciled under the original operation key before any
replacement is dispatched. Multiple messages, uploads, devices, or payment
notifications \MUSTNOT{} multiply the authorized economic effect.

The deployment \MUST{} define the evidence required to admit a paid request.
A local accounting entry alone is not evidence of external payment. Authorization,
capture, settlement, dispute exposure, and refund confirmation are distinct
facts. In particular, an authorization is not assumed to last throughout the
recipient decision window. The C-SQD profile requires authenticated evidence that the charge satisfies its
funding-finality policy before submission. A pending provider acknowledgment is
insufficient. Later reversals remain possible and require explicit recovery;
``finalized funding does not mean that dispute exposure has vanished.

means recording an amount due against the original charge and scheduling its
external execution, not claiming immediate receipt by the sender's bank.

Rejection \MUSTNOT{} establish a payment obligation to the rejecting recipient.
Pending forfeitures become eligible for the C-SQD pool process only under the
financial profile's maturity rules. Refundable amounts \MUSTNOT{} be counted
as distributable resources, regardless of where cash is held.

\subsection{The ledger and the outbox}

The ledger \MUST{} use append-only double-entry accounting, checked integer
amounts, and conservation within each settlement unit. It \MUST{} distinguish
processor clearing, unresolved request amounts, refund obligations, processing
revenue, utility-service receipts, pending forfeitures, corporate allocations, restricted member funds,
and finalized member payables. These are accounting categories, not general
user spending balances. Refunds and member payables \MUSTNOT{} fund another
request, transfer between users, or become on-demand wallet withdrawals.

A terminal request transition \MUST{} atomically commit its relationship effect,
any lane granted by bounded acceptance,
request outcome, ledger obligations, scheduled-work changes, and external-effect
intents. Workers execute those intents after commit. A payment or refund remains
pending until authenticated provider evidence and reconciliation establish its
completion. Failed or delayed payment \MUSTNOT{} erase the obligation.

Provider notifications \MUST{} be authenticated, associated with the correct
operation and amount, and deduplicated. Out-of-order evidence cannot regress a
confirmed outcome. A late capture of a cancelled request creates or updates the
full refund obligation; it cannot revive submission. Later external reversals
require separately authorized compensating entries, not rewritten protocol
history or automatic deductions from member entitlements.

\subsection{Funding attempts and service coverage}

The C-SQD host \MUST{} bound new funding attempts under operator-controlled
source verification and unresolved-attempt capacity. A failed or reversed funding
source requires restriction and authenticated re-verification; bank-account
replacement policy remains deferred. A queued reservation is not dispatch
authority: the host \MUST{} check current restrictions transactionally before
granting a new dispatch. Creating a
new request identifier \MUSTNOT{} bypass that restriction. Reconciliation of one
uncertain operation preserves its identity and does not consume a new attempt.

Annual utility billing belongs to the host service, separately from the request
lifecycle. Published terms fix a year-long service interval, price, and collection
date well before that interval starts. Failed or late retries \MUST{} retain
those terms and \MUSTNOT{} shift or extend the interval. Finalized funding
authorizes coverage only within that interval, without retroactive service. Before
issuing terms, creating or submitting a request, or admitting a follow-up,
accepted message, or lane message, C-SQD checks the recipient's local coverage and
the sender's coverage if the sender also has a local account. The remote sender's
provider owns its own entitlement check.

Pending annual funding grants no new coverage. Expired coverage \MUSTNOT{}
prevent recipient decisions, refund reconciliation, inspection of obligations,
or payment of a finalized member allocation. It does not revoke a relationship
or lane. An annual allocation is paid automatically as one lump sum to the
associated verified bank account. Its rebate and excess amounts are reporting
classifications, not separate processor operations. Distribution \MUSTNOT{}
revoke purchased service, and next-year renewal or funding \MUSTNOT{} gate or
reclassify it. Eligibility, annual allocation, and the distribution due date
remain governed by the financial profile. The C-SQD profile assumes one
configured payment-processing arrangement.

\section{Commands Along the Request Lifecycle}

Each command carries authenticated authority, expected versions, an idempotency
key, and domain-separated signing context. Exact replay returns the recorded
result; the same key with a different payload fails. Infrastructure retries
\MUST{} preserve the original intent and query an uncertain outcome before
creating another operation.

\subsection{Issue terms and create the request}

\texttt{IssueRequestTerms} checks relationship state, current request, recipient
policy, and principal-scoped history. Class-aware terms must additionally bind
the sender's selection to the recipient's published class and collateral. A blocked pair is refused. Accepted ordinary
communication requires no request charge. An existing nonterminal request is
returned as such; only an eligible new request receives a charge quote.

\texttt{CreateRequest} validates the quote signature, expiry, unit, versions,
initial-message binding, and sender authorization. The class-aware extension must
preserve the selected class and its terms. Under the directed-pair
uniqueness boundary it creates a \texttt{PreparingSubmission} request, fixes its terms,
schedules submission timeout, and records one external payment intent. It creates
no recipient solicitation and does not advance request history.

Stale terms, blocking, or standing acceptance prevent a new charge. So does an
existing request in preparation or awaiting a decision. If an external operation was already in flight when such a state
change occurred, its completion is reconciled and any resulting captured amount
is refunded through the cancelled request rather than silently abandoned.

\subsection{Submit the request to the recipient}

\texttt{SubmitRequestToRecipient} validates current permission, request and quote versions,
finalized funding evidence, submission and message-validity deadlines,
authenticated declarations, and durable encrypted content. The lifecycle becomes
\texttt{AwaitingRecipientDecision}. That transition \MUST{} atomically record the
single decision deadline, advance principal-scoped new-request history, replace
submission timeout with expiry, and store the initial delivery intent and one
logical solicitation effect.

If cancellation or timeout wins first, submission cannot revive the request. If
standing acceptance occurs while it is preparing, cancel the request and return its full
captured amount; the message may subsequently use accepted-sender admission.
Blocking before submission cancels it in full and prevents delivery. No message
becomes visible before its durable admission commits.

\subsection{Admit a follow-up}

\texttt{AdmitFollowup} identifies the exact request awaiting a decision and authenticates a new
message under the same directed pair. At one canonical boundary it checks that
the request is still awaiting a decision, the pair is not blocked, current recipient
follow-up policy permits the message, and content and declarations are valid.
It consumes permitted allowance once, associates the message, and records the
delivery intent. It leaves charge, financial deadline, and new-request history
unchanged.

Policy updates and follow-up admission \MUST{} serialize on a version or
equivalent guard. A request that closes first cannot accept a follow-up under
stale authority. If standing acceptance now permits delivery, the sender may use
that path with authenticated admission; a failed follow-up must not silently
become a newly charged request.

\subsection{Accept with standing permission or an express lane}

\texttt{AcceptRelationship} is authorized by the recipient for the directed
public-identity pair. It establishes accepted permission. If the current request
is awaiting a decision and the decision was timely, the same transition marks it accepted,
records exactly one full refund obligation, closes the solicitation, and cancels
expiry. If the request is preparing, acceptance cancels it in full instead.

A late acceptance grants future permission but cannot reopen a logically expired
or terminal request. Its expiry obligations must still be materialized. Accepting
one public identity \MUSTNOT{} accept sibling identities or reset the private
principal's new-request history.

For a submitted request still awaiting a decision, the recipient may accept by
granting an express lane. The transition \MUST{} commit the recipient-authorized
lane, accepted request outcome, full refund obligation, closure of the
solicitation, and cancellation of expiry together. It \MUSTNOT{} establish
standing acceptance. The lane concerns the relationship, may be scoped by purpose,
time, or volume, and is usually not scoped to a particular conversation.
Its subsequent expiry or revocation \MUSTNOT{} reopen the request's settlement.

A separate lane-management grant has the same effect on a pending request.
During preparation it cancels submission and voids or refunds the full charge.
A submitted request still pending receives the full refund even after its deadline;
already-terminal settlements remain fixed. The lane governs future access without
a new bond, subject to its scope and validity.

\subsection{Reject, block, unblock, and revoke}

\texttt{RejectRelationship} declines the current unaccepted relationship request.
A timely request awaiting a decision becomes rejected with the allocation defined above; a
preparing request is cancelled in full. Rejection closes the solicitation and
preserves history and applicable retry cooldown. It is invalid for an accepted
relationship, which must first be revoked. A rejection received after the request
deadline does not replace its expiry allocation.

\texttt{BlockRelationship} may operate from any relationship state. It sets the
pair to blocked, invalidates capabilities, and closes any solicitation. A timely
request awaiting a decision uses the rejection allocation; a preparing request is cancelled in
full; a logically expired or terminal request keeps its existing outcome. An
exact replay moves no further value.

While blocked, terms, creation, initial submission, follow-ups, and capability
admission \MUST{} be refused. \texttt{UnblockRelationship} returns the pair to
rejected without accepting it, restoring a lane, erasing history, or reopening a
request. \texttt{RevokeRelationship} changes accepted permission to revoked for
future contact without altering prior request outcomes. New requests after either
transition remain subject to current eligibility and recipient policy.

\subsection{Expire or cancel}

\texttt{ExpireRequest} acts only on a request awaiting a decision after the decision window
and journal holdback rules permit finalization. It rechecks timely decisions,
records expiry with processing revenue and collateral refund, closes the
solicitation, and schedules the refund. It \MUSTNOT{} undo a later prospective
acceptance. A stale job targeting a terminal request has no further effect.

\texttt{CancelRequestSubmission} may be authorized by the sender, a verified
pre-submission failure, or submission timeout. It marks the request cancelled,
voids or refunds its payment, cancels timeout, and creates neither a solicitation
nor a history increment. Cancellation is valid only before submission. Closing a
client or losing a network connection is not itself a cancellation command.

\section{Ordering, Retry, and Abuse Continuity}

Creation, submission, follow-up policy changes, and recipient decisions \MUST{}
serialize for the directed pair. Decisions carry an expected relationship
version; the first valid committed decision changes it and stale conflicting
decisions fail without partial effects. Bounded acceptance must include its lane
in that same decision boundary. Submission and cancellation share the
request version. No execution may both advance request history and cancel the
same request before submission.

Timely receipt must survive queue delay. A worker cannot win an expiry race merely
by running before a decision already durably received by the deadline. Conversely,
a new late decision cannot retroactively change the request's financial outcome.
Subsequent processing of an older request must preserve current permission.

Request history is keyed by $(P_i,I_j)$ through a scoped assertion. It contains a
new-request level, eligibility time, policy version, and canonical event position.
Initial submission advances it atomically; follow-ups and cancelled preparation do
not. Standing acceptance bypasses that history for the accepted public identity without
erasing it for aliases. Cross-identity request creation \MUST{} observe the shared
history boundary so concurrent aliases cannot all use stale initial terms.
Changing request classes \MUSTNOT{} reset or partition that history.

The deployment defines cooldowns after expiry and rejection, recipient overrides,
and explicit invitation rules. Any decay or forgiveness of history requires a
versioned policy event. Blocking remains effective regardless of willingness to
pay. These controls limit renewed requests; follow-up limits govern messages
inside the current one.

\section{Authenticated Envelopes and Scoped Capabilities}

The native envelope contains a versioned opaque message identifier, scoped
parties, creation and validity times, encrypted-content reference, declared
purpose, accountable origin declaration, optional payload-schema identifier, and
the request or permission proof needed for admission. Every admission-relevant
field \MUST{} be authenticated and bound to the encrypted content.

Purpose and origin use constrained, versioned values, including explicit
unspecified and legacy values. Unknown noncritical schemas may remain opaque;
unsupported critical extensions fail before admission. The envelope \MUSTNOT{}
expose plaintext, legal identity evidence, unrelated identities, financial
balances, or content-derived classifications unnecessary for the chosen path.

The reference deployment supports express lanes as recipient-signed scoped
capabilities. Each grant \MUST{} bind its subject and recipient, declared-purpose
constraint, optional origin constraint, acceptable declaration authority,
hard validity interval, enforceable count or rate, version, and revocation rules.
An opaque handle identifies the pair; it does not supply authority without the
grant. Admission compares authenticated declarations exactly under versioned
matching rules. It does not verify the semantic truth of encrypted content.
The request class records the proposed approach; the lane separately states the
permission actually granted. A class label is not itself a capability or a
lane-purpose constraint.

For legacy senders, a DMARC pass under RFC~9989 is evidence of authorized use of
the visible author domain, not proof of purpose, safety, company identity, or
human authorship. The gateway \MUST{} identify itself as declaration authority
and use legacy/unspecified origin unless separate authenticated evidence supports
more. The lane binds a recipient-selected canonical IDNA-ASCII domain scope,
accepting the domain or its authorized subdomains under a consistent versioned
mapping. A passing DKIM or SPF identifier cannot silently replace that scope.

The admission order is blocked refusal, accepted permission, valid capability,
permitted follow-up on the identified request awaiting a decision, then eligible new-request
creation with explicit sender authorization. If a request already exists but a
follow-up is refused, the last branch is unavailable. Capability mismatch never
silently converts a message into a charged request.

Capability-authorized delivery creates no request, forfeiture, solicitation, or
new-request history increment. Acceptance with a lane settles the request and
grants the lane atomically; later lane changes do not reopen that settlement.
Independent issuance during a pending request remains an open interaction.
Blocking invalidates capability authority; unblocking
does not restore it. The lane's activity clock cannot extend a request deadline
or establish distribution-program activity by itself.

\section{Security and Privacy Requirements}

Operational signing keys must be rotatable without changing the private
principal. Recovery and revocation \MUST{} prevent a compromised device from
retaining accepted or capability authority. Neither recovery nor distribution
verification authorizes provider access to native content keys.

\subsection{Native Content Confidentiality}

A conforming native client \MUST{} encrypt message content before transmitting it
to a provider. Only sender-authorized and recipient-authorized endpoints \MAY{} hold
the ordinary content-decryption keys. A provider, ledger operator, content store,
or delivery worker \MUSTNOT{} require possession of those keys as a condition of
native delivery.

The cryptographic binding between the encrypted content and its authenticated
envelope \MUST{} prevent substitution, truncation, reordering, and attachment
replacement. The recipient \MUST{} be able to authenticate the sending protocol
identity and detect an unexpected operational-key change. Implementations
\SHOULD{} provide auditable key transparency or an equivalent mechanism that
makes silent key substitution detectable.

Content storage \MUST{} use ciphertext references rather than embedding content
in relationship or ledger events. Server-side indexes \MUSTNOT{} contain plaintext
subjects, bodies, attachments, or content-derived terms. Search over native
content \MUST{} occur at an authorized endpoint or through a construction that
does not disclose the searched plaintext to the provider.

An SMTP gateway or other legacy bridge \MAY{} necessarily encounter plaintext or
provider-readable content. Such delivery \MUST{} be identified as outside the
native content-confidentiality guarantee. A provider \MUSTNOT{} represent ordinary
TLS transport or provider-managed encryption at rest as native end-to-end
encryption.

\subsection{Pairwise Relationship Privacy}

The logical relationship remains $(I_i,I_j)$, but components that do not route or
display an address \MUST{} represent it by an opaque pairwise handle $H_{ij}$. The
handle \MUST{} be generated randomly or by a secret-key construction with domain
separation and at least 128 bits of effective unpredictability. It \MUSTNOT{} be a
plain, unsalted, or otherwise publicly testable hash of the two protocol
identities.

A handle is scoped to one directed identity pair and one service domain. The
handle used for $I_i$ contacting $I_j$ \MUSTNOT{} reveal the handle used for the
same sender contacting another recipient. The reverse relationship from $I_j$ to
$I_i$ \MUST{} use a distinct handle. Relationship, content, ledger, and
observability systems \SHOULD{} use separately scoped references so that one
global identifier does not join all records without passing through a controlled
translation boundary.

The private new-request relationship $(P_i,I_j)$ similarly \MUST{} be
represented outside the identity service by a recipient-scoped opaque assertion.
That assertion \MUST{} allow the correct anti-persistence history to survive changes
of sender address, key, or provider, while preventing unrelated recipients from
comparing assertions to discover a common private principal.

A provider migration \MUSTNOT{} export a globally stable principal identifier.
Where continuity across providers is supported, the prior identity authority
\MUST{} instead issue or authorize a migration proof scoped to the recipient and
the new-request purpose. The receiving provider \MUSTNOT{} be able to use
that proof to discover the principal's relationships with other recipients.

There \MUSTNOT{} be a public or ordinary administrative interface that enumerates
the global relationship graph. A recipient \MAY{} inspect its own visible
relationships, and a sender \MAY{} inspect its own attempts and receipts, but neither
party receives another participant's unrelated edges. Pending, rejected, ignored,
revoked, blocked, and accepted relationships receive the same graph-confidentiality
protection.

An accepted relationship \MAY{} persist as an opaque handle or recipient-issued
capability together with current state, version, and revocation information. The
system \MUSTNOT{} retain the complete formation history merely because current
permission remains active.

\subsection{Compartmentalization and Minimal Disclosure}

The implementation \MUST{} separate identity evidence, relationship state,
encrypted content, financial settlement, and operational telemetry into distinct
access domains. This is a separation of authority as well as storage. Access to
one domain \MUSTNOT{} automatically grant access to the keys or translation data
of another.

Within ordinary operation, the identity service \MAY{} know that a verified
principal controls a public identity, but it need not receive message content.
The relationship service \MAY{} know $H_{ij}$ and its state, but it need not receive
legal identity evidence or plaintext content. The ledger \MAY{} know opaque account,
request, and settlement references together with amounts, but it need not receive a
subject, content reference, public address pair, or private principal identifier.
The content store \MAY{} know a ciphertext object and a scoped retrieval reference,
but it need not receive a request outcome or readable endpoint identities.

The delivery boundary necessarily learns enough to route to $I_j$ and to present
$I_i$ to the recipient. It \MUST{} disclose no additional principal evidence,
unrelated identities, balance information, or relationship history. A recipient
provider \MUST{} verify the applicable request admission, follow-up authority,
or capability without receiving unrelated financial activity. External providers
\SHOULD{} receive only the request charge, original-payment refund, or member
payment references and amounts needed for execution. They do not need message
content, follow-up events, or a correspondence graph.

Translation among scoped references is itself sensitive processing. Every
translation \MUST{} be authorized for a specific protocol purpose and recorded in
an access audit that does not reproduce the translated graph. Bulk translation,
graph export, and use of relationship data for advertising, sale, or unrelated
profiling are outside the protocol purpose and \MUSTNOT{} be enabled by default.

\subsection{Membership and Obligation Privacy}

Distribution verification \MUST{} use its own scoped member references, access
roles, and retention policy. An eligibility assertion may establish one qualifying
member without exposing legal evidence to delivery or the allocation engine.
Activity evidence \MUST{} be limited to facts needed by the published rule;
message content and unrelated correspondents are not eligibility inputs.
Unpaid refunds and finalized payables retain necessary payment/audit evidence
under their own retention classes even after ordinary message linkage expires.

\subsection{Metadata Leakage and Traffic Analysis}

Pairwise handles do not conceal every observable fact. Timing, network addresses,
message sizes, uncommon request amounts, and synchronized journal events can permit
correlation without a direct identifier. Implementations \MUST{} document which
of these signals each actor can observe and \MUST{} retain them only while needed
for routing, security, settlement, or a disclosed obligation.

Where it does not compromise correctness, an implementation \SHOULD{} reduce
correlation through coarse timestamps, payment batching, content
padding, separation of network and account logs, and short retention of delivery
telemetry. These protections \MUSTNOT{} alter quoted amounts, obscure a settlement
from its owner, or make request finality ambiguous.

\subsection{Record Lifecycle and Retention}

Every stored field \MUST{} belong to a documented retention class stating its
purpose, access domain, controlling key, operational lifetime, dispute lifetime,
any separately applicable required-retention period, and deletion method. A vague
interest in future analytics is not a protocol purpose for indefinite retention.

While a request is pending, the protocol \MAY{} retain the scoped linkage required
to authenticate admission, prevent replay, apply new-request policy, receive
a decision, and settle the request. Once the request is terminal and its defined
dispute period ends, the implementation \MUST{} remove fields and indexes that are
not needed for continuing permission, accounting, fraud handling, or an applicable
retention obligation. A durable accounting record \SHOULD{} contain only an
opaque settlement reference, amount, settlement unit, terminal state, canonical
time, and the minimum ownership evidence required for audit.

Continuing acceptance does not justify continuing retention of individual contact
attempts. After acceptance, the live relationship \MAY{} retain $H_{ij}$, current
state, version, capability or key references, and revocation material. Formation
messages, quote details, delivery telemetry, and superseded relationship events
\MUST{} follow their own shorter retention schedules unless independently needed.

Records retained solely for a legal, accounting, fraud, or dispute obligation
\MUST{} be segregated from online delivery and product analytics. They \MUST{} use
separate encryption keys, narrowly authorized access, an explicit expiration
condition, and an audit trail. A hold on one record or account \MUSTNOT{} silently
extend retention for unrelated users or relationships.

Deletion \MUST{} cover primary storage, replicas, caches, search indexes,
telemetry, derived datasets, and backups according to a documented propagation
schedule. If immediate removal from an immutable backup is impracticable, the
backup \MUST{} remain inaccessible to ordinary processing, expire within a fixed
period, and lose access to deleted record keys no later than that period.

\subsection{Cryptographic Erasure and Consent-Locked Archives}

Encryption is not erasure while the operator retains both a usable key and enough
linkage to identify the parties. When a retention period ends, the implementation
\SHOULD{} delete the unnecessary record and destroy every operator-held key that
could recover its identifying linkage. A claim of cryptographic erasure is valid
only when replicas, wrapped key copies, recovery escrow, and backups can no longer
restore that linkage.

The protocol \MAY{} support a consent-locked historical archive in which
reconstruction requires independent sender and recipient key shares. Such an
archive \MUST{} be separate from live permission and settlement state. Refusal or
loss of a share \MUSTNOT{} prevent the protocol from delivering under current
permission, applying revocation, enforcing backoff, or completing a pending
settlement.

If an implementation offers two-party reconstruction, it \MUST{} state precisely
which fields it covers and whether any separately retained compliance record can
identify the transaction without both shares. It \MUSTNOT{} promise two-party-only
reconstruction for information that the operator knowingly retains under an
independent decryption key. Required records that cannot be consent-locked \MUST{} be
minimized and isolated under the preceding retention rules.

No deletion or threshold scheme can erase a message or receipt independently kept
by a sender, recipient, endpoint backup, or external provider. The protocol's
guarantee concerns records controlled by conforming providers and \MUST{} describe
that boundary accurately.

\subsection{Operational Logs and Analytics}

Logs \MUSTNOT{} contain plaintext content, private identity evidence, raw key
material, full account balances, or a reusable global relationship identifier.
Debugging and tracing identifiers \MUST{} be scoped and rotated so that an
observability system does not become an accidental relationship graph.

Analytics \SHOULD{} operate on aggregates produced after the shortest useful
window. A dataset is not anonymous merely because names and addresses have been
removed; if scoped handles, rare amounts, precise timestamps, or auxiliary data
permit practical reidentification, it remains protected relationship data and
\MUST{} follow the corresponding access and retention rules.

\section{Incentives and Policy Authority}

The operator receives processing revenue on rejection or expiry and, under the
C-SQD profile, a disclosed share of eligible forfeitures. Recipients do not earn
a personal rejection bounty, but member duplication, fabricated activity, and
operator manipulation remain relevant risks. Providers \MUST{} make acceptance
available, issue signed decision receipts, and preserve timely decisions rather
than manufacture expiry. Pricing and financial-program policies \MUST{} include
review, dispute, correction, and audit procedures.
Recipients judge whether an approach fits their published class. Misrepresentation
may lead to ordinary rejection; this model adds no separate misclassification
penalty or reputation mechanism and does not guarantee truthful declarations.

Protocol policy fixes request economics and deterministic transitions. Admission
policy evaluates authenticated envelopes, permission, recipient follow-up
settings, rate/history state, funded local service coverage, and authorized safety
evidence. Presentation policy
runs for the recipient and controls ranking, notifications, digests, and local
content processing. Neither latter policy may rewrite charge terms or terminal
outcomes. A learned or probabilistic recommendation is not an authenticated
relationship command. Admission decisions \MUST{} reduce to versioned, auditable
rules over authorized inputs.

Payment does not bypass content or safety controls, and those controls do not
authorize decryption of native content. Providers may use recipient reports,
endpoint evaluation, metadata, and deliberately disclosed evidence within their
authorized purposes. Sender-facing errors should explain actionable constraints
without exposing unrelated recipient rules or enabling enumeration.

\section{Interoperability and Migration}

Versioned exchanges \MUST{} cover identities, scoped continuity assertions,
request terms and identifiers, payment/admission evidence, message envelopes,
capabilities, recipient decisions, outcome receipts, and financial obligations.
The class-aware extension must bind the selected class and distinguish the
permission granted on acceptance; exact representations remain implementation work.
Equivalent canonical inputs must yield equivalent request outcomes. Signatures
and idempotency domains \MUST{} prevent reuse across deployments and versions.

Federation must preserve scoped references, native encryption, request uniqueness,
history continuity, and financial finality. It cannot assume that old direct
recipient-collateral clearing applies to the company pool. A separate agreement
must assign charge/refund responsibility, pool governance, membership uniqueness,
clearing, default losses, and payment obligations before real federated value is
enabled. SMTP downgrade must be visible before transmission and in the envelope.

\section{Required Invariants and Conformance Stories}

Conformance \MUST{} preserve the following together:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item One nonterminal request per directed pair, one authorized charge per request,
      across all classes, and no additional financial effect from follow-ups or retries.
\item One request deadline fixed at submission; timely-decision priority and no
      retroactive reopening of a terminal or logically expired request.
\item Full return before submission; no ordinary cancellation after submission.
\item Atomic permission, request outcome, ledger obligations, and outbox intent,
      with external confirmation represented separately.
\item Exact outcome allocation, integer value conservation, and no recipient
      entitlement created by rejection.
\item No distributable use of refundable amounts, no repeated corporate assessment
      of carryforward, and no disappearance of unpaid finalized obligations.
\item Directed public-identity permission with private principal history continuity,
      including concurrent aliases, without public identity linkage.
\item Blocking overrides every admission authority; unblocking does not accept,
      restore capabilities, erase history, or reopen money.
\item Authenticated declarations, current follow-up authorization, replay-safe
      allowance consumption, and message-validity isolation.
\item Native content confidentiality, pairwise scoping, compartmentalization,
      bounded retention, and honest erasure across replicas and backups.
\end{enumerate}

A reference suite should begin with one request and three permitted messages:
each acceptance mode creates one full refund obligation and its stated permission.
The extension must exercise fixed class terms, class-switching attempts, atomic
lane acceptance versus conflicting decisions, and lane expiry without reopening
settlement. These are required extension scenarios, not claims about existing tests.
The suite should also
exercise disabled follow-ups, concurrent creation, alias rotation, cancellation
versus submission, a late capture after cancellation, timely decisions delayed in
queues, late acceptance, duplicate and out-of-order provider evidence, and failed
refund recovery. Profile tests must additionally exercise maturity exclusions,
once-only corporate allocation, annual eligibility, integer rounding, zero
eligible members, carryforward, and failed member payments. They must also cover
advance collection for fixed annual service intervals, unchanged terms across
failed or late retries, and equal allocations independent of renewal. For
$U=\$120$, allocations of \$80, \$120, and \$170 must each produce one bank
payment, with reporting values $R_i=\min(D_i,U)$ and $X_i=\max(0,D_i-U)$.
Tests must cover automatic payment to the associated verified bank account,
one completion lifecycle with lost-response recovery, and payment after closure.
Future renewal funding must not delay or reclassify a distribution. The earlier
full service charge remains separate, and distribution does not revoke coverage.
Pending funding must grant neither request submission nor new service coverage.

The implementation sequence follows the same story: model request transitions
and obligations; establish the transactional journal and outbox; add authenticated
payment evidence and reconciliation; integrate encrypted delivery and follow-up
controls; then validate the C-SQD financial program with simulated external
effects. Live rails and federation follow only after their deployment policies
are explicit. The desktop pilot may use a nonredeemable simulator without
claiming that it has validated live payment operations.
